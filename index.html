BUSINESS NEED & STRATEGIC CASE

A strategic move beyond the CBD.

Oakwood has identified growing demand for boutique-style accommodation in Melbourne’s lifestyle-oriented suburbs. Guests increasingly want an intimate, personalised stay without compromising the service standards Oakwood is known for.

The expansion will strengthen Oakwood’s competitive position, reach a new guest segment, generate additional revenue and extend the brand into new suburban markets. It will also diversify Oakwood’s accommodation offering and improve accessibility for guests.

LOCATION SHORTLIST

Three promising Melbourne destinations.

Each location is assessed against accessibility, customer demand, local lifestyle appeal, competition and long-term growth potential.

View detailed location analysis ↗
01

Melbourne CBD

Melbourne’s strongest and most stable hotel market, supported by corporate, international and leisure demand throughout the year.

  • Demand and performance: Highest expected occupancy and room-rate potential, but also the strongest hotel competition.
  • Access and workforce: Excellent train, tram, bus and airport-transfer connections, supported by Melbourne’s largest hospitality labour pool.
  • Facilities and growth: Extensive offices, retail, dining, theatres and attractions, with continued commercial, tourism and public-realm investment.
  • Key challenges: Highest property and operating costs, limited suitable sites, complex heritage and planning controls, delivery congestion and some overlap with Oakwood’s existing Southbank market.
02

Docklands

A modern waterfront and transport precinct serving corporate, event and leisure guests, with direct access to Southern Cross Station.

  • Demand and performance: Stadium, concert and event periods can lift occupancy and rates, although demand may soften during quieter non-event periods.
  • Access and operations: Excellent access to trains, airport coaches and trams, with strong road connections and newer buildings supporting regular deliveries.
  • Sites and development: Potentially more affordable than the CBD, with larger development sites, modern mixed-use projects and stronger hotel-conversion potential.
  • Strategic value: Expands Oakwood into western Melbourne, supported by Marvel Stadium, waterfront renewal and good access to corporate and event customers.
03

Carlton

A distinctive inner-city destination supported by university, medical, cultural and extended-stay demand.

  • Demand and performance: Potentially stable university, medical and longer-stay demand, although the conventional hotel market is smaller.
  • Facilities and workforce: Close to the University of Melbourne, major hospitals, Melbourne Museum, the Royal Exhibition Building and Lygon Street, with access to student and hospitality labour.
  • Strategic value: Provides the strongest customer diversification and the lowest direct overlap with Oakwood’s existing Southbank property.
  • Key challenges: Less direct airport access, limited suitable hotel properties, heritage and neighbourhood restrictions, and reduced loading and storage capacity in older buildings.

HR & WORKFORCE PLAN

Building the team in three phases.

Recruitment will be aligned with construction so employees are appointed at the right time. Existing Oakwood employees will be considered for transfer or promotion before external recruitment.

View detailed workforce plan ↗
PHASE 01

Confirm the operating structure

Validate departments, leadership roles, staffing ranges and recruitment priorities against the hotel’s capacity, facilities and service model.

PHASE 02

Appoint hotel leadership

Recruit the Hotel Manager and department heads first so they can finalise procedures and help select their operational teams.

PHASE 03

Assemble the operational workforce

Prioritise roles requiring longer recruitment or training, followed by supervisors and frontline employees closer to opening.

OPENING ASSUMPTION

≈130 employees at opening

This is a planning estimate and will be adjusted as the hotel design, occupancy and service requirements are confirmed.

Staffing principles

  • Keep essential 24-hour services permanently staffed.
  • Use flexible staffing for variable demand.
  • Allow workforce capacity to grow with occupancy.
  • Consider outsourcing for specialist activities.

OUTSOURCING APPROACH

Control stays with the hotel.

Outsourcing may be considered for laundry, security, specialist maintenance and temporary staffing. Decisions will consider total cost, service quality, flexibility, compliance and operational control—not labour cost alone. An accountable hotel manager and measurable service standards will remain in place.

Opening-ready from day one.

The proposed expansion will operate as an upscale, cashless hotel and serviced-apartment property. Operational planning will ensure the right inventory, equipment, storage, suppliers and systems are ready before opening.

Inventory and equipment

Linen, toiletries, cleaning products, food and beverage stock, apartment starter packs, housekeeping equipment, payment terminals, meeting technology and maintenance tools.

02

Storage and deliveries

Separate storage will be required for linen, food, chemicals, waste, luggage and meeting equipment, supported by a dedicated loading dock, service entrance and goods lift.

Commercial linen laundry will be outsourced. A small internal maintenance team will be supported by specialist electrical, plumbing and air-conditioning contractors.

Payments, reception systems, Wi-Fi, CCTV, key-card access, emergency procedures and fitness-room security will be tested before guests arrive.

RISKS & CONTINGENCIES

Preparing for a controlled opening.

Key risks will be managed through early decisions, backup arrangements and a phased approach to recruitment, training and operations.

01

CUSTOMER SERVICE

Consistent guest experience

Risk: A new team may deliver inconsistent service during the opening ramp-up.

Buffer: Complete structured induction, service simulations and employee shadowing before opening.

02

HR & WORKFORCE

The right people at the right time

Risk: Recruitment delays, capability gaps or hiring too early or too late.

Buffer: Assess Oakwood employees first, recruit critical roles early, maintain backup candidates and align hiring with construction.

03

LOGISTICS & OPERATIONS

Reliable opening operations

Risk: Limited loading or storage, incorrect stock, supplier disruption and unresolved overnight security.

Buffer: Confirm suitable delivery access, use backup suppliers, maintain essential buffer stock and finalise the security model before opening.
